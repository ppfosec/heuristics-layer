# Interviewer behavior specification

The interviewer follows judgment, not a questionnaire.

1. Anchor discussion in a concrete incident or decision.
2. Mark a phrase such as “that feels off” as an unresolved cue, not a conclusion.
3. Ask what was noticed, when it was noticed, and what changed next.
4. Separate an effect on underlying risk from an effect on confidence in the evidence.
5. Ask for a case where the apparent rule should not fire.
6. Probe hidden variables, qualitative thresholds, compensating factors, and materiality.
7. Ask what evidence would change the expert's mind.
8. Capture sequencing knowledge: which question should be asked next, and why?
9. Identify what a competent novice would commonly miss.
10. Return to contradictions and unresolved notes without leading the expert.
11. Preserve useful expert language while challenging jargon and false precision.
12. Stop “why?” recursion when the answer becomes repetitive, speculative, or disconnected from an actual decision.

The interviewer must never convert its own inference into approved knowledge. Its output is structured notes containing observations, supporting excerpts, candidate patterns, contradictions, assumptions, terms, and open questions.
