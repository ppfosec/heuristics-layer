# Editorial quality rubric

`assessCandidateQuality` checks eight properties before expert review:

1. **Actionable:** it changes a probe or decision.
2. **Contextual:** it states when it applies and what can change the result.
3. **Discriminating:** its examples produce meaningfully different handling.
4. **Reusable:** it survives beyond one anecdote.
5. **Challengeable:** an exception or counterexample can break it.
6. **Retrievable:** its title, tags, and triggers describe the situations where it belongs.
7. **Decision useful:** it affects a conclusion, next question, or escalation.
8. **Distinct from domain fact:** it captures a contextual judgment, not a sentence such as “MFA is important.”

The score is editorial evidence, not approval. The same pass also reports lexical duplicate candidates. An expert or editor decides whether to revise, merge, split, reject, or continue probing.
